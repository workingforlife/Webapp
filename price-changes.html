How the Tracker Works

Every day we download the open-source model price catalog and compare it with the previous day's copy. Anything that differs is recorded with the date we first saw it: a changed input, output or cache-read price, a changed context window, a model that appeared, or a model that disappeared. The log starts on the date shown at the top, so earlier changes are not included.

What It Cannot Tell You

  • The date a provider changed a price. It shows when our catalog first showed the change, which can be later than the announcement.
  • Prices you actually pay. It tracks list prices per million tokens. Discounts, committed-use deals, free tiers and regional pricing are not covered.
  • Why a model was removed. "Removed from list" means it is no longer in our catalog. It may have been retired, renamed or dropped by the catalog.
  • Mistakes in the catalog. The catalog is community maintained, so a change may be a correction, and a change can be reversed a few days later. Check the provider's own pricing page before you rely on a figure.

How to Use It

  • Filter to price cuts and increases to see what really moved, and to new models for fresh launches.
  • Search for a model you use to check whether its price or context window has changed.
  • Take a change into the Model Picker or Cost Forecaster to see what it does to your monthly bill.

Questions

How often is it updated?

Once a day. The date it was last checked is shown at the top of the page.

Why do some changes list several models?

Providers often publish the same model under more than one name, such as a dated version and a short alias. When several names change in exactly the same way on the same day, they are shown as one change.

Can I get alerts?

Not yet. For now, check this page, or filter to a provider and bookmark the link you get from Copy Link.

Where does the data come from?

From an open-source model price catalog, which we download daily. The history on this page is our own record of how that catalog changed from day to day.
