How the Model Picker Ranks Models

For every model in our price list, it works out what your request would cost: input tokens times the input price, plus output tokens times the output price, divided by a million. It then multiplies by your monthly volume and sorts. Models that cannot hold your request in their context window are left out. That is all it does, on purpose.

What It Does Not Do

It does not score quality. Price tells you what a model costs, not how well it handles your task, and any quality ranking we invented would be a guess. Use the picker to cut a long list down to a shortlist of three to five models, then test those on your own examples before you choose.

How to Use the Results

  • Start with your real token sizes and volume, or one of the example workloads.
  • Add your current model to see what each alternative would save or add.
  • Open the four cheapest in Model Prices to compare them side by side, or take the shortlist into the Cost Forecaster for chatbots and agents whose cost grows with history. To price one real prompt on every model, see how much a prompt costs on every model.

Assumptions

  • Cache: if you enter a cache share, that part of the input is charged at the model's cache-read price where our list has one, and at the full input price where it does not. Cache-write charges and the cache lifetime are not included, so treat cached figures as a best case.
  • Batch: the discount you enter is applied to the whole cost. Not every provider or model offers batch pricing, so check before you rely on it.
  • Prices come from an open-source catalog and are refreshed daily. Provider pages are the final word.

Questions

Does the cheapest model give the best value?

Not necessarily. A cheaper model that needs retries, longer prompts or human fixing can cost more overall. That is why the picker only builds a shortlist. Test the shortlist on your own task.

Why are some models missing?

Models whose context window is smaller than your request are hidden, as are models with a zero input or output price, because their real cost is not in the price per token. Audio, search, research and similar specialised models are hidden unless you tick the box in the filters.

Does it include reasoning or thinking tokens?

Only if you include them. Reasoning tokens are usually billed as output, so add them to the output tokens per request.

Is anything I enter uploaded?

No. The page downloads the price file and does all the arithmetic in your browser. Your numbers are only kept in the link if you copy it.

Where do the prices come from?

From an open-source model price catalog, refreshed daily. The date the catalog was last refreshed is shown at the top of the page. It is not checked against provider billing.
