What It Is For

Long prompts are re-sent on every request, so every wasted word is paid for again and again. This tool finds filler, repeats and wordy phrases, shows each change, and lets you decide what to keep. Then it turns the token saving into a monthly figure in the Cost Forecaster.

What It Finds Besides Wording

Some token costs are not words at all. Invisible characters, emoji, lines broken in the middle of a sentence by a PDF, and long tracking codes on links all add tokens without adding meaning. In our tests they cost noticeably more than the plain version. The myths guide shows the measured numbers, and the checks here are built from it.

What to Expect

Rule-based trimming is modest. On verbose, human-written prompts, savings of roughly 10 to 30 percent are typical. Bigger cuts usually need a human to merge overlapping instructions or shorten examples.

Frequently Asked Questions

Will the trimmed prompt behave the same?

Not guaranteed. The tool never changes numbers, links, {{variables}}, quoted text, code, tags, or anything inside tags such as <example> and <document>, and it checks that they are all still there. But wording can matter to a model, so test the shorter prompt on a few real inputs before switching.

Why are some trims off by default?

Words like "important", "make sure" and "very" can signal emphasis. Removing them may weaken an instruction, so they are off until you choose.

What are hidden characters, and are they all removed?

Zero-width spaces, soft hyphens, byte-order marks and non-breaking spaces often arrive when you copy from a web page or a document. They look like nothing, or like a normal space, but still cost tokens. The tool removes those, and turns a non-breaking space into a normal one. It leaves zero-width joiners and non-joiners alone, because emoji sequences and several scripts, including Hindi and Persian, depend on them.

Why is emoji removal off by default?

Emoji often cost more tokens than the word that could replace them, but they can carry tone or meaning, so you decide. The same goes for bold markers.

What does the tracking-code rule change?

It removes well-known tracking parameters from links, such as utm_source, fbclid and gclid. The page a link opens is almost always the same, but a few sites use such parameters for sign-in or discount codes, so look at the change before you keep it.

How does it decide that lines were broken by a PDF?

It looks for blocks of three or more lines of similar length with no list markers or indentation, which is how copied PDF text usually looks, and joins them into one paragraph. Lists, code and short lines are left alone. Check the diff if your text is poetry or a table.

Is my prompt uploaded?

No. Everything runs in your browser. Nothing is sent anywhere.

Why might the saving be smaller than expected?

If your system prompt is cached, the repeated part is already billed at a reduced rate, so shrinking it saves less. The Cost Forecaster models this when you turn caching on.
